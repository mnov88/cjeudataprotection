A0.3: SABAM, a copyright management society, sought a court order requiring Scarlet, an internet service provider, to install a filtering system to block its customers from sharing copyright-protected musical works via peer-to-peer networks without authorisation. Scarlet challenged the injunction as requiring general monitoring of all network communications and infringing EU law on data protection, freedom of expression, and freedom to conduct business.

Note: This case does not directly concern Article 6(1)(f) GDPR legitimate interest. It is included because it establishes a foundational balancing-of-fundamental-rights framework (intellectual property vs. freedom to conduct business, data protection, and freedom of information) that is highly relevant to the legitimate interest doctrine under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. The Court's proportionality and fair balance reasoning prefigures the balancing test applied under legitimate interest assessments.

A5.2: The Court held that EU directives (2000/31, 2001/29, 2004/48, 95/46, and 2002/58), read together in light of fundamental rights, preclude an injunction requiring an ISP to install a general filtering system covering all electronic communications of all customers, for an unlimited period, at the ISP's own expense. The injunction failed to strike a fair balance between copyright protection on one hand and the freedom to conduct business, the right to data protection, and freedom of information on the other. While this case does not address Article 6(1)(f) GDPR directly, the Court's balancing framework — weighing the legitimacy and proportionality of measures against competing fundamental rights — is a direct precursor to the legitimate interest balancing test.
