A0.3: A Latvian driver (B) challenged national legislation that made penalty points imposed for road traffic offences, recorded in the national vehicle register, publicly accessible to any person requesting them without needing to demonstrate a specific interest. The Road Safety Directorate (CSDD) disclosed this data to anyone providing the driver's national identification number, and commercial re-users also obtained and disseminated the data.

NOTE: This case is not decided under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest) but primarily under Article 6(1)(e) (processing necessary for a task carried out in the public interest) and Article 10 GDPR (data relating to criminal convictions and offences). It is included because the Court performed a detailed balancing of public interest (road safety, transparency) against fundamental data protection rights, applying proportionality and necessity analysis that is structurally analogous to and doctrinally relevant for the legitimate interest balancing test under Article 6(1)(f).

A5.2: The Court held that the GDPR, in particular Article 5(1), Article 6(1)(e), and Article 10, precludes national legislation obliging a public body to make penalty point data accessible to any person without requiring the requester to establish a specific interest. While road safety is a recognised objective of general interest, the necessity of unrestricted public disclosure was not established because less restrictive means exist and the regime goes beyond what is needed, with the fundamental rights to privacy and data protection prevailing over the public interest in access.
