A0.1: C-597/19
A0.2: 2021-06-17
A0.3: Mircom, a company holding intellectual property rights assigned by film producers, sought to compel the internet service provider Telenet to disclose the identification data of customers whose IP addresses had been recorded by Media Protector while those customers allegedly shared copyrighted films on a peer-to-peer network using the BitTorrent protocol. Telenet and other ISPs opposed the request, questioning Mircom's standing and the lawfulness of the IP address collection under the GDPR.

A1.1: Y
A1.2: Obtaining personal information of persons who allegedly damaged property (intellectual property) in order to sue them for damages | Recovery of claims by an assignee of intellectual property rights
A1.3: Obtaining personal information of persons who allegedly damaged property in order to sue them for damages > qualifies as a legitimate interest, supported by Article 9(2)(e) and (f) of Regulation 2016/679 | Recovery of claims by an assignee > may constitute a legitimate interest justifying the processing of personal data under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR

A5.2: The Court held that Article 6(1)(f) GDPR, read in conjunction with Article 15(1) of Directive 2002/58, does not in principle preclude either the systematic recording of IP addresses of peer-to-peer network users or the communication of their names and postal addresses to the rightholder for civil claims, provided that such initiatives are justified, proportionate, not abusive, and have their legal basis in a national legislative measure under Article 15(1) of Directive 2002/58. The Court left it to the referring court to verify whether such national legislative measures exist and to carry out the actual balancing of interests in the specific circumstances.
