A0.3: A trolleybus operated by Rīgas satiksme was damaged when a taxi passenger opened the car door, scraping the trolleybus. Rīgas satiksme sought the passenger's personal data (identity document number and address) from the national police in order to bring a civil action for damages, but the police refused to disclose the full information.

A1.1: Y
A1.2: The interest of a third party in obtaining personal data of a person who damaged their property in order to sue that person for damages

A1.4: N
A1.5: Case C-275/06 Promusicae > "the interest of a third party in obtaining the personal information of a person who damaged their property in order to sue that person for damages can be qualified as a legitimate interest" > Pursuing compensation for property damage through civil proceedings is a legitimate interest

A5.2: The Court held that Article 7(f) of Directive 95/46 does not impose an obligation to disclose personal data to a third party to enable civil proceedings, but it does not preclude such disclosure on the basis of national law. The Court confirmed that suing for property damage is a legitimate interest and that obtaining the address or identification number is necessary when a name alone is insufficient for identification, while the balancing of interests depends on the specific circumstances of the case.
