A0.3: The KNLTB, a Dutch tennis federation, disclosed personal data (names, addresses, dates of birth, phone numbers, email addresses) of its members to two sponsors — a sports products company (TennisDirect) and a gambling provider (NLO) — for advertising and marketing purposes, in exchange for remuneration, without obtaining members' consent. A5.1: unclear
A5.2: The Court held that a commercial interest of a sports federation in disclosing members' personal data for consideration to sponsors for marketing purposes could constitute a legitimate interest under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR, provided it is lawful, but that processing is only permissible if strictly necessary (with no less restrictive equally effective alternative available) and if members' fundamental rights do not override that interest. The Court strongly suggested that informing members and seeking their wishes beforehand would be a less intrusive alternative, and that members likely would not reasonably expect their data to be sold to sponsors, particularly to a gambling provider with no relevant connection to tennis.
