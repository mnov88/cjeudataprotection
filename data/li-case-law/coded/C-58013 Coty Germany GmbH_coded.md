A0.3: Coty Germany, a perfume producer holding an exclusive licence for the Community trade mark Davidoff Hot Water, purchased a counterfeit perfume via an internet auction platform and paid into a bank account at the Stadtsparkasse. When Coty Germany asked the bank for the name and address of the account holder to pursue intellectual property enforcement, the bank refused by invoking banking secrecy.

NOTE: This case does not directly concern Article 6(1)(f) GDPR legitimate interest. It concerns the balancing of fundamental rights under Directive 2004/48 (Enforcement of Intellectual Property Rights), specifically the right to information (Art. 8 of the Directive), the right to an effective remedy (Art. 47 Charter), and the right to intellectual property (Art. 17(2) Charter) against the right to protection of personal data (Art. 8 Charter) and banking secrecy. It is included because the Court's balancing analysis is structurally analogous to the legitimate interest balancing test under Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.

A5.2: The Court held that Article 8(3)(e) of Directive 2004/48 precludes a national provision that allows, in an unlimited and unconditional manner, a banking institution to invoke banking secrecy to refuse to provide information concerning the name and address of an account holder suspected of IP infringement. Such a blanket refusal seriously impairs the fundamental right to an effective remedy and the right to intellectual property, failing to ensure the fair balance between competing fundamental rights that EU law requires.
