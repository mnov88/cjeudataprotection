A0.3: SNCF Connect, a French rail ticket seller, required customers to select a title ("Mr" or "Ms") when purchasing travel documents online. The association Mousse complained to the French data protection authority (CNIL) that this mandatory collection of title/gender identity data lacked a lawful basis under the GDPR and violated data minimisation. The CNIL dismissed the complaint, and Mousse challenged that decision before the Conseil d'Etat, which referred questions to the CJEU.

A5.2: The Court held that processing of title data for personalising commercial communication based on gender identity cannot be regarded as necessary for the controller's legitimate interests where customers were not informed of the interest pursued, where the processing is not strictly necessary (as personalised communication can rely on names alone or generic expressions), or where data subjects' fundamental rights prevail due to a risk of discrimination on grounds of gender identity. The Court strongly suggested that the necessity and balancing conditions were not met in this case, effectively indicating that legitimate interest was not a valid basis for this processing.
